Big-screen readout — wiring

A bench prototype built from spare parts: an ESP32-C3 scans WiFi + BLE and streams "draw this row" commands over one wire to an Arduino UNO, which paints a 3.5" TFT shield and logs to its microSD. This page shows exactly what connects where. Firmware lives in v2/proto_readout_c3/ and v2/proto_readout_uno/.

  • Link one-way UART, C3 → UNO
  • Just 2 signal wires (data + ground)
  • Power 2-port USB power bank
  • Baud 19200 (Option A) · 9600 (Option B)

How it all connects

The C3 and UNO are powered separately from the two ports of one power bank, so the only wires between the boards are the data line and a common ground. The TFT shield plugs straight onto the UNO.

data (C3 TX → UNO RX) common ground 5 V power (from bank)

The link — just two wires

Because each board draws power from its own port on the bank, nothing but the data line and a shared ground crosses between them. The C3's 3.3 V TX drives the UNO's 5 V RX fine — no level shifter needed this direction.

A 2-port power bank already shares ground internally, so the boards' grounds are tied through it. The dedicated GND wire is belt-and-suspenders for a clean signal — keep it.

Two ways to reach the UNO's RX

The TFT shield covers every UNO header pin. You need access to a receive pin. Pick one approach.

Recommended · keeps SD

Option A — free up D0

The shield's D0/D1 pins are dead (connected to nothing — they only use D2–D9, A0–A4, D10–D13). So there's no trace to cut; just get the dead D0 pin out of the UNO's socket.

  1. Find D0 — the two pins nearest the USB connector are D0 (RX) and D1 (TX). You only touch D0.
  2. Bend the shield's D0 male pin outward (away from the USB shell), or snip it flush. It's dead, so this is harmless.
  3. Re-seat the shield. The UNO's D0 socket is now free.
  4. Plug C3 GPIO21 into D0, and C3 GND into ICSP pin 6.

Keeps: hardware Serial (most reliable), microSD logging, 19200 baud. No code change.

No shield changes · drops SD

Option B — use ICSP (D12)

The ICSP data pins are just D11/D12/D13 — the same bus the microSD uses. You can borrow MISO (D12) as a SoftwareSerial RX only if you give up the SD card.

  1. Plug C3 GPIO21 into ICSP pin 1 (MISO / D12).
  2. Plug C3 GND into ICSP pin 6 (GND).
  3. Firmware changes: UNO uses SoftwareSerial on D12, parks the SD (drive D10 HIGH, never call SD.begin); both boards drop to 9600 baud.

Trade-off: no SD logging (display only), and SoftwareSerial is less tolerant than the hardware UART while the TFT is drawing. Ask and I'll hand you the modified sketches.

Why ICSP can't give hardware serial: the ATmega's only UART RX is D0, and it isn't brought out to the ICSP header. ICSP is SPI + power + reset only.

Power

Run both boards from the 2-port USB power bank for now — one port to the UNO's USB jack, one to the C3's USB.

PortFeedsDraw
1UNO + 3.5" TFT (backlight ~100–150 mA)~200 mA
2ESP32-C3 (WiFi/BLE bursts)~100 mA avg, 300 mA peak

Solar later, not now. A bare mini panel can't drive the backlight directly. To add solar you need a battery buffer + charger: a 5–6 V ≥2 W panel → CN3065/TP4056-solar charger (or an IP5310 board) → 18650 → 5 V out → both boards. Solar then trickle-charges while the battery does the work.

Flashing & libraries

ESP32-C3

Board: ESP32C3 Dev Module, USB CDC On Boot: Enabled. Flash over the C3's own USB. BLE + WiFi come with the core.

Arduino UNO

Libraries: MCUFRIEND_kbv + Adafruit GFX Library (SD + SPI are built in). microSD CS = D10 on most shields — verify on yours.

Before uploading to the UNO: unplug the C3 → D0 wire. D0 is the USB serial RX; leaving the C3 driving it makes the upload fail. Reconnect it after flashing.
